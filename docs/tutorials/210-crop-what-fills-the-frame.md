Choose which part of a recording fills your output frame, by moving a box over it.

## Steps

1. Select a clip on the timeline. {show: clip on:video}
2. Switch the monitor to [Source], or press `V`. {show: view}
3. Drag the box to move it, and drag its corners to resize it. {show: monitor-view on:video "Drag the box on the Source view"}
4. For an exact figure, type [X], [Y], [Width] and [Height] in [Crop]. {show: crop-x on:video}

## Details

The Source view shows the whole recording with a box over it. That box is the part of the
recording that fills your output frame.

Typed numbers move the picture as you type. The up and down arrow keys step a number by two
pixels, twenty with `Shift`. A left or top edge typed past where the box would fit keeps the
number you typed and makes the box narrower or shorter instead. One visit to a number is one
undo, and `Escape` puts back what it was.

`Alt` with an arrow key nudges the box two pixels. Add `Shift` and it moves twenty. That is the
difference between lining a face up properly and giving up.

On a clip that zooms, the box and the numbers are the crop at the playhead, and changing them
changes the zoom keyframe there, or makes one. See Zoom in and out during a clip.

This is how a landscape recording becomes a vertical video without squashing anything: you are
not stretching the picture, you are choosing which part of it to keep. Source above 1080p is
worth having for exactly this reason, because a vertical reframe throws away most of the
horizontal pixels.

On a clip with more than one picture, see Choose which picture you are framing.
