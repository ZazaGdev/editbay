Blend one sound into the next where two clips meet on an audio track.

## Steps

1. Point at the top of the cut between two sound clips. {show: sound-clip on:sound "Point at the top of the cut beside this clip"}
2. Press its [Add a transition here] button. {show: sound-clip on:sound "Point at the top of the cut beside this clip, then press its button"}
3. To change its kind, right click the crossfade and choose [Even], [Linear] or [Smooth]. {show: clip-transition on:transition "Right click the crossfade, then choose its kind"}

## Details

Sound takes transitions the same way picture does, and on an audio track the kinds are the ones
that matter for listening rather than looking. They are named for what they sound like rather
than for the maths behind them:

- **Even** keeps two crossfaded sounds at the same loudness all the way through. It is the
  default and it is the one to reach for. Premiere calls this Constant Power.
- **Linear** fades one down and the other up in a straight line. Premiere calls this Constant
  Gain, and it usually dips in the middle.
- **Smooth** fades away steeply at first and then gently. Premiere calls this Exponential Fade.

If you are joining two pieces of the same recording, Even is almost always right. Linear is
what you want when you specifically need the dip, and Smooth suits a sound tailing off to
nothing.

A crossfade on sound is added and shaped exactly like one on picture, so everything in the
Cutting entries applies here too.
